The circular law for independent row slices down to logarithmic degree

Griffin P. Hurt / October 10, 2026 / v1.1-preprint

Read the paper ↗ (PDF, 69 pages) Explore the Lean proofs ↗ Zenodo & source archive ↗

Preprint; not peer reviewed. The sufficiently large absolute degree constant is unoptimized.

Abstract

Let Mn be an n × n random 0–1 matrix with independent rows, each uniform over vectors having exactly d ones. We prove that, for an absolute constant C* > 0, the empirical spectral distribution of

Mn / √(d(1 − d/n))

converges in probability to the circular law whenever C* log n ≤ d and d = o(n). This lowers the known log2+ε n threshold to sufficiently large logarithmic degree throughout the sparse range.

The main obstacle is the lack of column regularity, which removes the aggregate-profile identities available for random regular digraphs. In the lower polylogarithmic range, we replace those identities by an averaged multinomial row-profile estimate and combine it with a rectangular right-kernel analysis to control all but O(n/d2) of the smallest singular values. A regularized row-replacement argument then compares logarithmic determinants with those of an i.i.d. Bernoulli matrix. Above every fixed superlogarithmic cutoff, the same comparison is combined instead with a uniform polynomial smallest-singular-value bound. Girko hermitization and the sparse i.i.d. circular law complete the proof.
